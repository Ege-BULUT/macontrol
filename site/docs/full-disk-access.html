Full Disk Access

Full Disk Access is the one permission macontrol asks for. It lets the app measure the folders macOS keeps private.

Why it asks

macOS hides some folders from apps until you allow it: Mail, Messages, Safari, Cookies and the Trash, among others. Without access, a disk tool sees those folders as empty or smaller than they are. macontrol does not guess: it marks them “not scanned”, so a missing size never looks like a small one.

Access is for reading names and sizes. It does not switch off macontrol's own protections: the protected places stay protected. macontrol has no networking code, so nothing it reads leaves your Mac.

Turn it on

On first launch macontrol shows three steps:

  1. Open System Settings. The button opens Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access.
  2. Turn on Macontrol. If it is not in the list, drag the app icon from macontrol's sheet into the list, or click Reveal in Finder and drag the app from there.
  3. Come back. macontrol checks every second while the sheet is open and continues by itself once access is on.

If you switched it on and macontrol still says it is off, macOS may apply the change only after a relaunch. The sheet then offers Relaunch Macontrol.

To do it yourself later: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access, then switch on Macontrol.

What works without it

Everything. Click Continue Without Access and use the app as usual. A “Limited mode: Full Disk Access is off” banner lists the folders that are not scanned. Their sizes are missing, not zero, and anything inside them is left out until access is on.

After an update

Each release is signed ad hoc, and macOS ties Full Disk Access to the app's signature, so a new version has to be allowed again. If the switch is already on but macontrol says it is off, remove Macontrol from the list with the minus button and add it again.
