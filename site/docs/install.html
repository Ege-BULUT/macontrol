Install macontrol

Four steps. One of them is only needed the first time, because macontrol is not notarized yet.

You need a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Intel Macs are not supported yet.

Download

Download for Mac

You get {{file}} ({{size}}). To check that it arrived intact, compare its SHA-256 with the one below:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/Macontrol-*.zip

Expected: {{sha256}}

Unzip and move to Applications

  1. Double-click the zip in Finder. You get Macontrol.app.
  2. Drag Macontrol.app into your Applications folder.

Safari unzips downloads by itself while Open “safe” files after downloading is on in its General settings, so you may find Macontrol.app in Downloads already unzipped. Move it to Applications. To check the SHA-256 first, turn that setting off and download again.

Unzip with Finder, or with ditto -x -k in Terminal, not with the unzip command. unzip leaves ._ files inside the app, which breaks its signature, and macOS then calls the app damaged.

Open it the first time

macOS 15 Sequoia and later

  1. Double-click Macontrol in Applications. macOS says it could not verify that “Macontrol” is free of malware. Click Done.
  2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll down to the Security section. Next to “Macontrol” was blocked to protect your Mac, click Open Anyway.
  4. In the dialog that follows, click Open Anyway again and confirm with your password or Touch ID.

macOS remembers the choice, so later launches open normally. The Open Anyway button stays for about an hour after the blocked attempt. If it is gone, open the app once more and go back to Privacy & Security.

macOS 14 Sonoma

Control-click Macontrol in Applications, choose Open, then click Open in the dialog. The Open Anyway button in Privacy & Security works here too.

Why this step is needed

macOS checks apps downloaded from the internet with Gatekeeper. Apps that Apple has notarized open straight away. Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership, which macontrol does not have, so each release is signed ad hoc instead. The step above tells macOS that you trust this download. If you would rather not, you can build it from source.

Allow Full Disk Access

On first launch macontrol asks for Full Disk Access and walks you through it. It continues by itself once access is on. You can also skip it: the app still works, and the folders macOS keeps private show as “not scanned”. More about Full Disk Access.

Update to a new version

Quit macontrol, download the new zip, unzip it and replace Macontrol.app in Applications.

Each release is signed ad hoc, so macOS treats the new version as a different app and Full Disk Access has to be granted again. If Macontrol is listed and switched on but the app still says Full Disk Access is off, select it in the list, remove it with the minus button, and add it again.

Remove macontrol

Quit it and move Macontrol.app to the Trash. It keeps its undo journal in ~/Library/Application Support/Macontrol, and any settings in ~/Library/Preferences/dev.macontrol.Macontrol.plist. You can remove both, and take it out of the Full Disk Access list in System Settings.
